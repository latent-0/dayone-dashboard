Social does one job in this funnel: warm the named accounts that outbound is about to contact, and capture the ones who raise their hand with the keyword “MAP”. LinkedIn is the channel; everything else supports it. All copy here is draft for partner approval, and no figure in it is a claim about past results.

Channels and roles
  • Three partner profiles (primary). Personal voice, 3 posts a week. Sponsors follow people, not logos.
  • Company page. Repost, publish the monthly Operating Library newsletter, host the Value Map sample.
  • X (light). Re-cut the sharpest line of each post; 3 posts a week, no extra effort.
  • Podcasts and communities. One guest spot a month; the clip becomes a post.
Six content pillars
  • Thesis: Equity value = Earnings × Multiple
  • Six value dimensions: GTM, margin, NRR, pricing, defensibility, cost
  • Operator, not advisor: who owns the number
  • First 100 days: Diagnose and Operate in practice
  • Exit readiness: data room and buyer story from day one
  • Regional notes: what changes in Europe, what does not
Weekly rhythm and timing
  • Mon · Wed · Fri posts, 13:00–15:00 CET (7–9am ET) to catch both regions.
  • Thu poll or question (best for comments, which feed reach).
  • Put links in the first comment, not the post body.
  • Reply to every comment within a few hours; ask a question back.
  • Every second Wednesday, post at 16:00 ET so the US west coast sees it.
Daily 20-minute engagement routine
  • Comment thoughtfully on 10 posts from named target accounts and their sponsors (not “great post”).
  • Send 5 personalised connection requests; stay well under roughly 100 a week.
  • Reply to every comment and DM from the previous day.
Capture mechanics
  • “MAP” in comments: auto-DM with the Value Map sample link.
  • “SCORE”: DM with the portfolio scorecard link.
  • “BRIEF”: DM with the briefing booking link.
  • Every link lands on the operating-partner page with org, role and country fields.
Rules for every post
  • One idea, one next step, one voice.
  • No invented statistics or testimonials. Targets we state (for example NRR past 115%) are targets, not results.
  • Clients and sponsors are named only with written consent.
  • Hook in the first line: it is all that shows before “see more”.
  • Never tag or pitch in the comments of people you have not engaged with.
Measure weekly (read it from the dashboard and LinkedIn analytics)

ICP profile views and new ICP followers · keyword comments (MAP / SCORE / BRIEF) · captures per week and ICP share (in region, in segment) · briefings booked and which post or email preceded them. One metric to beat: keyword comments per week. Set targets after a four-week baseline.

#1 · Mon wk 1 · ThesisCTA: MAP
Equity value = Earnings × Multiple. Most software deals underwrite both numbers. Then the hardest part, actually operating the business to a bigger number, goes back to the team that was already running it flat. We built Dayone Ventures to close that gap. We operate, we don’t advise. The plan and the people who run it are the same team. Where would you look first for earnings in a $5–40M ARR software company: revenue, margin or product? Comment MAP and we’ll send a sample Value Map.
Link in first comment: Value Map sample page
#2 · Wed wk 1 · Six dimensionsCTA: MAP
Where value leaks in a lower-middle-market software company. Six places we look before we touch anything: 1. Go-to-market efficiency: where pipeline, conversion and payback quietly break 2. Gross margin and unit economics: the true cost to serve 3. Net revenue retention: churn, expansion, durability 4. Pricing and packaging: value captured vs value delivered 5. Product defensibility: the moat, and the debt underneath it 6. Cost and automation: where offshore and AI take work out of the P&L Evidence before capital. Rank every fix by what it is worth, then act. Want the one-page version? Comment MAP.
Also publish as a 1-slide carousel
#3 · Fri wk 1 · Operator, not advisorCTA: reply
Operating partner vs advisor: the difference is who owns the number. An advisor hands over a recommendation. An operating partner owns the plan and the outcome together, and stays in the room until the number moves. We would rather own the number than opine on it. If we are not accountable for the outcome, we are not the right partner. Sponsors: what does your current operating support actually own?
Reply to every answer within the day
#4 · Mon wk 2 · RetentionCTA: answer
Buyers underwrite net revenue retention harder than almost any other metric. Growth that depends on constant new logos has to survive diligence. Retention that compounds is easier to defend: onboarding and success loops, expansion that outruns churn, workflow lock-in. Our standing target for the companies we operate is NRR past 115%, held in balance with the Rule of 40 rather than traded against it. What is the first retention lever you would pull in the first 100 days?
“Target”, not a result: keep this wording
#5 · Wed wk 2 · First 100 daysCTA: MAP
The first 100 days after close decide most of the hold. A simple way to structure them: · Diagnose: a diagnostic across six dimensions, ending in a Value Map · Rank: every intervention valued, owners named · Operate: revenue, margin and product engines on one weekly cadence No capital moves ahead of the diagnosis. We turned this into a one-page template. Comment MAP and we’ll send it.
Carousel: one slide per step. Template = Value Map sample
#6 · Fri wk 2 · EuropeCTA: MAP / DM
Operating playbooks do not copy-paste from the US to Europe. What changes: pricing in EUR and GBP, several languages in product and support, GDPR and data residency, and markets that are fragmented country by country. What doesn’t: the six dimensions where value leaks, and the discipline of diagnosing before spending. If you back lower-middle-market software in the UK, DACH, Benelux, the Nordics or France, we’d like to compare notes. DM us or comment MAP.
Publish in the 13:00–15:00 CET window

Most software companies charge for what they ship, not for what it is worth. Pricing and packaging is the cleanest lever in a Value Map. Three questions to start: · Which plan do customers choose, and why? · Which feature would they pay more for? · When did you last change the price? What did you find the last time you looked?
